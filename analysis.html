An experiment. The scores and reasons here come from a language model reading only the headlines and RSS summaries of each article, not the full articles, and not checked by a person. They can be wrong or miss context. Use your own judgement: read the original articles and make up your own mind.
How the analysis works
  1. Read the feedsEach outlet's RSS headline and summary. The outlet's name is hidden in the text.
  2. Group the same storyArticles about one event are matched by their wording, one per outlet.
  3. Ask the modelThe group's headlines, summaries, outlet names and times go to an AI model, told to use only that text.
  4. Score and explainIt lists what the outlets agree on and how they differ, quoting the words, and gives a 0–10 spread score with reasons.
  5. Check and keepA reply that won't parse is sent back once. Each result is saved with the model and time.

What the score means

  • 0–2 Same tone: the outlets describe the event in much the same tone.
  • 3–4 Small differences: small differences in emphasis or wording.
  • 5–6 Clear differences: clear differences in word choice, emphasis or what is included.
  • 7–8 Loaded framing: loaded language, labels or omissions put one outlet far from the others.
  • 9–10 Sharply opposed: accusations, moral labels, or claims that one text contradicts another.

What the page adds

  • The phrases the model quoted are highlighted in the headlines and summaries, coloured by the kind of difference, so its reading can be checked against the text.
  • Outlet pairs: for two outlets that appear together in several groups, the median spread of those groups.
  • Left and right: outlets carry a rough lean (from AllSides and Ad Fontes Media ratings, as in the game). The model is never told it. Comparing groups that have both a left- and a right-leaning outlet with groups that don't shows whether its scores follow that axis.
  • Read twice: a story that grows while on the page is analysed again. How often the new reading lands in the same band as the old is a rough check on how steady the model is.
  • Polarized pairs are two-article rounds built from the most left- and most right-leaning outlets in a group, so they score higher by design. They can be shown apart from standard groups.

What it does not do

  • It doesn't open the articles or read beyond the headline and summary.
  • It isn't told any outlet's political leaning.
  • It can't see what an outlet left out of its feed, or context outside the text.
  • A high score means the framings differ, not which framing is right.

How much is analysed

Up to 40 groups that have no analysis yet are sent on each scheduled run, a few times a day. The rest wait for later runs.

Framing analysis

How the newsrooms frame the same story

For each group of articles about one event, an AI model reads the headlines and summaries, scores how far apart the framings are, and suggests possible reasons. Scores run from 0, where outlets describe the event in much the same tone, to 10, where the framings are sharply opposed.

These are readings of the text shown, not verdicts on any outlet. The model can be wrong, and "possibly" in a reason marks a guess. Outlet names are shown here, which is why this page is kept apart from the game until a story is over.
